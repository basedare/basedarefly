# Product reference lessons — researched 2026-10-11

These are proposed BaseDare experiments, not shipped features or verified competitor performance. The supplied Instagram reel could not be fetched (Instagram throttled it); the HumanPost analysis below uses its current official site and cannot be attributed to that exact reel.

## HumanPost: make the operation concrete

HumanPost describes manually performed social distribution with account setup, task progress, proof collection and performance reporting. Its managed service describes research, creative approval, distribution and iterative content tests. Those are vendor claims, not independently audited results.

BaseDare application: a venue should see a small, concrete campaign sequence: brief → accepted participants → evidence → reviewed result → settlement record. Test three approved creative angles with a bounded local creator group, each linking to the relevant place or activity. Measure qualified visits, opted-in joins and completed evidence, not just impressions. Keep paid promotion, display permission and commercial reuse explicit; real people alone do not prove authentic engagement. Do not copy account farms or undisclosed promotion.

Sources: [HumanPost product](https://humanpost.com/), [managed service](https://humanpost.com/services).

## Hinge: give people a reason to meet

Hinge explains specific profile prompts and comments as conversation starters. Its private We Met feedback asks about an offline date and whether someone would meet again. The useful pattern is lowering the first-message barrier and learning from offline outcomes.

BaseDare application: optional activity-oriented prompts such as “I’m up for…”, “My ideal two-hour plan…” and “I can help with…” on creator profiles; a contextual “Join for coffee / surf / a photo walk” invitation from an actual activity; a private post-plan question asking whether it happened and whether the person would do it again. Do not turn a self-report into a verified visit or financial proof. Consent and existing friend/block rules still govern contact. Rank a small set by explicit availability, activity interest and general area before attempting opaque compatibility scores.

Experiment: measure plan-page → RSVP, RSVP → self-reported meetup, repeat participation and unwanted-contact reports. Ask less during onboarding; learn interests from opt-in choices. No dating framing is needed.

Sources: [Hinge approach](https://hinge.co/mission), [We Met](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/360010692913-What-is-We-Met), [conversation starter feature](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/46735258688659-What-are-Convo-Starters).

## Unconventional AI: a sharp thesis backed by artifacts

Its homepage connects a focused computing thesis with dated research/model releases. Its [un]CFO article describes an internal source-linked research library, contextual retrieval and human review of AI-assisted work. This does not establish that its infrastructure or computing approach is appropriate for BaseDare.

BaseDare application: pair the street identity with a very clear promise—find something worth doing nearby—and publish evidence of progress: real place stories, completed activities, changelog and permissioned creator examples. A place page should connect source, timestamp, evidence type and latest status. PeeBear should distinguish a suggestion, usual venue pattern and confirmed scheduled activity. A lightweight internal knowledge system can connect venue facts, approved campaign lessons and release notes with their sources. Keep private profiles/location and commercial permissions outside public retrieval.

Design inference: use one distinctive brand idea and calmer space around the primary action. The expressive map and bear should carry the personality; text, status and financial values should stay easy to scan.

Sources: [Unconventional AI](https://unconv.ai/), [[un]CFO article](https://unconv.ai/blog/the-making-of-the-uncfo-building-a-company-in-the-age-of-ai/).

## Suggested order

1. Clear activity-oriented profile prompts and contextual invitations using existing people/friend flows.
2. A private, explicitly self-reported post-plan feedback loop.
3. A bounded venue content experiment with explicit rights, evidence and qualified conversion metrics.
4. Source-linked PeeBear/place recommendations and a simple public progress log.

These need their own product/data design and implementation pass; no new matching algorithm, outreach, campaign spend, feedback collection or AI integration was enabled by this visual release.
