# DareOS: the real-world operating layer

## Hypothesis

BaseDare can become the friendly consumer interface to a reusable real-world coordination layer: **DareOS**. DareOS is not a metaverse, a generic feed, or an immediate rebrand. It is the common grammar that lets a person turn an intention into a safe, shareable, time-bounded real-world plan.

The primitive is a **Plan Link**:

1. Pick an intention, place, and time.
2. Share one public link.
3. People join with the lightest useful commitment.
4. The plan exposes live state: who is in, what is still needed, and what happens next.
5. The group runs the activity.
6. The place keeps a durable, privacy-safe memory and makes repeating the plan easy.

Boat crews and **Meet me here** are the first two tests. They use the same handoff shape while preserving domain-specific rules: boat capacity, operator confirmation, and ability lanes remain boat concepts; venue meetups remain short-lived public invitations.

## Product rules

- BaseDare remains the approachable surface; DareOS can mature as the architecture and operator vocabulary.
- Browse and share before requiring identity. Require verified identity only to create or join when trust matters.
- The map is the feed. Plans should change visible place state without creating duplicate markers.
- Never expose precise live participant location. Share the public place and plan state.
- Keep the interaction short: intent → details → share. Advanced controls belong behind progressive disclosure.
- Haptics should confirm meaningful state changes—joined, crew ready, operator confirmed—not decorate every tap.
- Orchestration supports humans; it does not grant BaseDare autonomous authority over venues, boats, safety, or payments.

## Validation metrics

- Plan creation completion rate
- Share rate after creation
- Invite open → join conversion
- Time to minimum viable group
- Plan show-up/completion rate
- “Run it again” rate within seven days
- Host/operator response time and cancellation rate

Do not expand into chat, feeds, or broad social profiles until Plan Links demonstrate repeated real-world coordination.
